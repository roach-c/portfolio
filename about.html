About Me

Who I am when the
resume is closed.

The home page tells you what I make. This one is meant to tell you who is making it, where I came from, and what I’m actually trying to do with all of it.

01 · Introduction

Nice to meet you properly.

I’m Caleb Roach, a Communications and Design student at Southern Nazarene University, and the shortest honest description of me is probably that I have a hard time leaving a problem alone once I’ve noticed it.

I grew up in Arkansas, I moved to Oklahoma City for school, and somewhere in the middle of those two things I figured out that I like building things for people a lot more than I like talking about building things. Right now I work for a roofing company during the day, I’m a full time student at night, and I co own a small design studio with a friend on the weekends, which is honestly more than I should be doing at once, but it has also been the most I’ve ever learned in a single year.

What makes me a little unusual, I think, isn’t any one of those skills on its own. Plenty of people can shoot video and plenty of people can write code. The part I’m proud of is that I can sit in the first meeting, figure out what a business actually needs, and then go do the design, the writing, the build, and the launch myself without handing it off to three other people. That doesn’t make me the best at any single piece of it, and I know that, but it does mean nothing gets lost in the middle.

02 · How I Got Here

Arkansas, then Oklahoma, then one project that changed it.

I grew up in Arkansas with two brothers, Luke and Seth, and a couple of dogs, and I’d say most of what I know about working with other people I picked up in that house before I ever picked it up in a classroom. Sharing a room with brothers teaches you how to argue about something and still eat dinner together afterward, and that turns out to be a surprisingly useful skill when a client doesn’t like the first draft.

Moving to Oklahoma City for Southern Nazarene was the first time I was really on my own, and it took me longer to adjust than I expected it to. I started making videos because I liked making them and not because it was going anywhere. Then somebody asked if I’d make one for them, and then somebody else asked if I could build a website while I was at it, and I kept saying yes to things I didn’t fully know how to do yet, which is pretty much the whole story of how I got here.

If I had to name one turning point, it would be the first time I handed a finished website over to a real client and then watched them actually use it. Nobody was grading it, there was no rubric, and if it broke it was going to be my name on it. That was the moment this stopped being something I was studying and started being something I do, and it’s the reason I chose a major that sits right on the line between communication and design instead of picking one side of it.

03 · What I Value

Four things I try not to negotiate on.

A list of nice words doesn’t really mean anything on its own, so here is what each of these actually costs me when I follow it.

  • 01

    Integrity

    Doing the work the same way whether or not anybody is checking on it. In my line of work a client usually can’t tell the difference between a site I rushed and a site I took my time on, at least not for a few months, and I think that gap is exactly where character either shows up or doesn’t.

  • 02

    Follow Through

    I’d much rather tell somebody no up front than say yes and then go quiet on them. I’ve been on the receiving end of that, and it’s worse than a rejection, so now I’m careful about what I commit to and then I finish what I commit to.

  • 03

    Lifelong Learning

    Almost everything I get paid for right now is something I taught myself after a class ended. Nobody assigned me web development, and I don’t think the learning ever really stops in this field anyway, so I’ve tried to get comfortable being the person in the room who doesn’t know yet.

  • 04

    Service

    The point of any of this is the person on the other end of it. A website is not really the deliverable. A small business owner sleeping better because their leads stopped falling through the cracks is the deliverable, and keeping that straight changes what I build.

04 · What Motivates Me

Mostly the moment something finally works.

The honest answer is that I’m motivated by the moment a thing works. There is a very specific feeling when a site goes live, or when a piece of software I wrote quietly does its job in the background for somebody, and I’ve been chasing that feeling for a couple of years now. Getting paid is great, but it isn’t the part that gets me to stay up and finish something.

Underneath that, I think what really drives me is helping small businesses that can’t afford an agency. A lot of the companies I work with are run by people who are genuinely good at their trade and have been losing customers for years because their website looked abandoned, and being the person who fixes that is a much better motivator than a grade is. I also like being twenty and getting to prove that I can be trusted with real work, which is probably some amount of ego, but it keeps me moving.

And I like the puzzle. Most of what I do is really just translation, taking what somebody means and turning it into something a stranger can understand in four seconds, and I don’t think I’ll get bored of that.

05 · Outside The Work

When I close the laptop.

I play piano, and it’s the one creative thing I do that nobody is waiting on, which is probably why I’ve kept at it. I play pickup basketball when I can find a game, I follow the Razorbacks because I grew up in Arkansas and I didn’t really get a choice, and I follow the Thunder now that I live here. I took two and a half years of Spanish in high school and I’m taking it again at Southern Nazarene, mostly because I got tired of understanding about half of a conversation.

I also have two dogs, I take way more photos than I ever end up using, and I spend a fair amount of my free time building things that nobody asked for just to see if I can. Not all of those turn into anything, but a few of them have turned into the projects on this site.

06 · Walk of Faith

Where faith actually shows up.

Southern Nazarene is a Christian university, and faith is part of why I chose it, though I’d rather say I’m still working a lot of it out than pretend I have it all settled. I grew up around church, and for a long time that was mostly something I inherited, so figuring out what I actually believe on my own has been a slower and less dramatic process than the testimonies I heard growing up.

Where it shows up most is in how I treat people, and specifically in the idea that a client is not a transaction. When I’m sitting across the table from somebody, I try to remember that the project matters to them in a way it never will to me, and that changes how carefully I listen and how honest I am about what I can and can’t do. Integrity and keeping my word are really the two places where I’d say my faith touches my work more than anything I’d say out loud about it.

I’m not going to claim I’m consistent at it, because I’m not. But it’s the standard I’m aiming at, and going forward I’d like the way I run a business to be the argument rather than the way I talk about it.

07 · A Brief Look Ahead

Where I’m pointed.

As I finish my degree and keep taking on real work, I want to end up somewhere that lets me keep doing both halves of this, the storytelling and the building, instead of making me pick one. I’d like to keep growing the studio, keep getting better at the parts I’m weakest at, and be the kind of person a team can hand something to and stop worrying about it.
