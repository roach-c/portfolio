Why I picked this major

I went back and forth between communication and something more technical for a while, and the reason I landed where I did is that I kept noticing the same problem from both directions. A business with a great story usually can’t get it built, and a business with a working website usually can’t explain what it does. Communications and Design sits right on that line, so instead of picking a side I picked the seam between them, which is where I’d already been doing all my work anyway.

What the program has taught me

The most useful thing has been learning to write for somebody who isn’t me. Media writing and public speaking both come down to figuring out what a person actually needs to hear and then cutting everything else, and that turns out to be the same skill as designing a home page, because a visitor gives you about four seconds either way. The design side taught me the visual half of that, and the business courses gave me the vocabulary I needed to sit in a real meeting and talk about scope and price without guessing.

The other half of my education has been the jobs. School gave me the framework and then a paying client gave me the deadline, and I don’t think either one would have worked as well without the other.

How it’s preparing me

Almost everything I do at work right now is a direct application of something I’m studying, which is not something I expected to be able to say this early. The writing courses show up in the copy on a client’s home page, the design courses show up in the layout, and the business courses show up in the proposal that got the project approved in the first place. Having a full time job in the field while I finish the degree means I get to test what I learn against a real company almost immediately, and the parts that don’t hold up in practice are the ones I go back and study harder.

The career I’m aiming at

Long term I want to keep running a studio that builds websites and internal software for small businesses, because that is the work I’ve enjoyed most and the clients I most want to help are the ones who can’t afford an agency. Short term I’m looking for the kind of role where I’m responsible for a company’s whole digital presence rather than one slice of it, something along the lines of a marketing and web specialist, a digital media manager, or a creative lead on a small team.

What I’d want out of either path is the same thing. I want to stay close enough to the work to still make it myself, and close enough to the client to know whether it actually helped.
