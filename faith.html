My Walk of Faith

A faith I’m still
growing into.

I would say my faith is less of a separate part of my life and more of the thing underneath the rest of it. This page is my attempt at explaining how it actually shapes the way I work, the way I lead, and the way I treat people.

01 · My Faith Journey

Making it my own.

Faith has been around me for most of my life, but I think having it around you and actually owning it are two pretty different things. Choosing Southern Nazarene was part of that for me, since I wanted to be at a Christian university where faith is part of the conversation in class and not something saved for the weekend.

College has been where I’ve had to start sorting out what I believe for myself instead of what I was handed, and that has been a slower and quieter process than I expected it to be. I don’t have all of it settled, and I’d rather say that than pretend I do, but I would say my faith is more my own now than it was when I got here.

What has helped the most is being around people who take their faith seriously without making a show of it. Watching how they handle a hard week or a disagreement has taught me more than most of what I’ve read, and it’s a big part of why I care more about how I live this out than how I talk about it.

02 · Faith In Practice

Where it actually shows up.

I think values only mean something once they change what you do, so here are the six places where my faith has the most say in my decisions.

  • 01

    Integrity

    To me integrity means doing the work the same way whether or not anybody is checking on it. In school that means carrying my share of a group project even when nobody would notice if I didn’t, and at work it means telling a client the truth about what something will cost and how long it will take, even when a different answer would be easier to give.

  • 02

    How I Treat People

    I try to treat the person in front of me like they matter more than whatever I need from them. A client isn’t a transaction, and neither is a classmate or a coworker, so I try to listen first, stay patient when something isn’t going well, and be honest even when it’s a little uncomfortable.

  • 03

    Leadership

    I’m not super experienced as a leader yet, but leading the video side of the Weekly News and co owning a studio have both taught me that leading mostly means serving the people you’re working with. I think a good leader takes the blame, shares the credit, and does the boring parts of the job without being asked.

  • 04

    Service

    A lot of the businesses I work with are run by people who are really good at their trade and can’t afford an agency, and I see helping them as a way of serving and not only a way of getting paid. I want the skills I’ve been given to be useful to somebody other than me.

  • 05

    Decision Making

    When I have a hard decision to make, I try to ask what I’d be comfortable explaining to anybody who asked, and not only what I could get away with. That question has talked me out of saying yes to more than I can handle, and it’s usually the one that slows me down enough to make the right call.

  • 06

    Staying Teachable

    I get things wrong pretty often, and my faith is a big part of why I’m okay admitting that. I’d much rather own a mistake and fix it than defend it, and I think being teachable is worth more than looking like I already have it figured out.

03 · Faith & My Career

How I hope the two meet.

Going forward I want to own and run a web design company, and I hope my faith shows up in how that company operates more than in anything written on its website. To me that looks like fair prices, honest timelines, keeping my word, and treating the people who work with me well.

I also don’t think success means much on its own. I want to do well, and I’m not going to pretend I don’t, but I’d like whatever success I have to come with a commitment to serving other people and giving something back to the community I’m in.

I fall short of this pretty regularly, and I think being honest about that is part of it. It’s still the standard I’m aiming at though, and I’d rather people be able to see it in my work than have to take my word for it.

04 · What I’ve Learned

Three lessons I keep relearning.

  • Character is what you do when it costs you something.

    Being a person of character, to me, means your values still hold when they’re inconvenient. It’s easy to be honest when honesty is free, and I think the real test is what you do when it costs you time, money, or an argument you’d rather not have.

  • Most good things grow slowly.

    My faith, my business, and my relationships have all grown a lot slower than I wanted them to. I’ve learned that rushing them usually makes them worse, and I’m still learning to be okay with that.

  • People come before the project.

    Deadlines matter, but I’ve never regretted slowing down for a person. I would say that’s the lesson I have to relearn the most often, since I tend to get locked in on the work.
