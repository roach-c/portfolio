TODD, a Voice Controlled Robot

  • CourseManagement Information Systems (MGT 4113)
  • WhenFall 2026 · in progress
  • TeamFour people
  • My partSpeech and response programming

Our team is building a robot from parts, with no kit, that you can talk to. TODD (Technology and Operations Delivery Droid) listens for spoken commands, drives where you tell it, and answers simple questions out loud, all on a budget of about forty dollars. The point of the project is to see how an information system actually works when the data comes in as a voice instead of a form.

I’m responsible for the speech side. It runs on a used Raspberry Pi 3B+ with an offline speech recognizer for the drive commands, so moving the robot never depends on campus WiFi, and only open ended questions go out to an AI model for an answer. I also make the weekly design sheets, like the one here.

  • Raspberry Pi
  • Python
  • Speech recognition
  • Systems design
  • Budgeting
  • Technical writing

Outcome so far Part I, covering our objectives, feasibility, cost, and build plan, is finished. The robot gets demonstrated live in December.

What I learned The hard part of a robot isn’t the code, it’s the constraints. A school network that blocks devices from talking to each other changed our whole design before we wrote a single line.

Hand Lettering Showcase: Words That Inspire

  • CourseTypography (GDSG 2433)
  • WhenSpring 2026
  • TypeIndividual final project
  • ToolsProcreate, printed and mounted

The final for Typography was to hand letter a phrase that means something to you. I picked “Don’t count the days, make the days count,” then ranked every word by importance so the ones that carry the meaning (don’t, days, and count) got the biggest and heaviest letterforms.

I limited myself to two or three styles, a blackletter and a thick script, and built most of the letters by hand in Procreate on top of reference fonts I traced and then changed until they were barely recognizable. The two “days” sit side by side in the middle, so they got their own decorative roman and blackletter hybrid.

  • Hand lettering
  • Type hierarchy
  • Procreate
  • Print production

Final product A finished hand lettered poster, printed and mounted.

What I learned Hierarchy decides everything. Once I knew which words mattered most, the layout basically drew itself.

Hamlet Book Cover

  • CourseDesign I (GDSG 1213)
  • WhenFall 2024
  • TypeIndividual final project
  • ToolsAdobe Illustrator, hand illustration

For the Design I final I redesigned the full jacket for Hamlet twice, once text heavy and once image heavy. Most Hamlet covers show him holding the skull for “to be or not to be,” but the story is really about revenge, so on the front I drew a hand gripping the skull through its eyes instead, which reads a lot less like a thoughtful prince and a lot more like the character actually is.

I drew the illustrations myself, then set the jacket in a bold red serif on a muted grey so it feels closer to a magazine cover, and made the spine the loudest part so the title reads from across a room.

  • Adobe Illustrator
  • Illustration
  • Layout
  • Concept development

Final product Two complete book jackets, one text heavy and one image heavy, plus a written design rationale.

What I learned The first idea is usually everybody’s first idea. Looking at what every other cover did is what pushed me somewhere better.

Pumped Poultry Logo System

  • CourseDesign I (GDSG 1213)
  • WhenFall 2024
  • TypeCreative brief and logo
  • ToolsAdobe Illustrator

The assignment was to reimagine a chicken logo, so I wrote my own creative brief first. Pumped Poultry is a made up gym chain that always opens next door to a chicken restaurant, aimed at trendy gym goers, and it needed a mark that works on an app icon as well as a sign.

I went with a sharp, angular rooster and fast italic lettering so the softness of a chicken turns into something that looks like it lifts, then built out the full set: color, one color, reversed, and a monogram for small sizes.

  • Logo design
  • Creative briefs
  • Brand systems
  • Adobe Illustrator

Final product A creative brief and a full logo system in color, one color, reversed, and monogram versions.

What I learned A logo isn’t one image, it’s a set of them. Writing the brief first made every one of those versions easier to decide on.
